After A busy Summer just trying to settle in a new place, I started work on my vegetable garden. This has been a dream for me, planting some nice flowers and growing my own food.

That being it took much longer than I wanted, and also because some crops are best planted in that season, but despite some setbacks, I´ve managed to get some stuff done.

I found out that Autumn is actually a decent season to start planting, as a nice Lady told Autumn is where everything thrives, I guess living with a Portuguese Mediterrean climate also helps. Without further ado here´s my progress.

To start things off I had to start clearing an area of weeds since this place hasn´t been cared for in a while, I also had to plow the soil to aerate it. Then added horse manure that serves as a natural fertilizer, since we are going for a biologial garden over here.

And finally some of the beds were elevated with a lower level intervaled between them to help with the water drainage.


![A clean and empty patch of soil](/images/2026/gardenpreparation.jpg)


Next is the covering, which helps the soil maintain its humidity longer, so I dont have to water it as much and also prevents weed growth. The work alone having to just remove them and without chemicals? This way takes less effort and I only to focus on any rogue menace that shows up close to my plants.

![An empty garden bed with a black covering](/images/2026/gardencover.jpg)

After all that, its now time for some planting. At first, I was going for seeds, but because we were getting to Autumn I decided to just get transplants. The advantage of this is by being already established that I have better control of when I get to plant them instead of waiting for first sprouts.

![A bunch of lettuce sprouts on a small black planting tray](/images/2026/alfacetransplante.jpg)

And time is of the essence here. I got this from a local fair which they have every week.

Mind you, I didn´t got them all of once, just little by little, see the most important part of my plan was picking up what is best to plant this season and where.


Ever heard of plant coassociation? If you want a biological garden with minimum pesticide use, this is how you do it. Basically some species grow better when planted close to certain ones, while others have to be avoided or at least keep their distance. 

This way it prevents the spread of pests and diseases as well aid in their growth.

![A bunch of plants in a row](/images/2026/plantrow.jpg)

To Give an example Carrots and Garlic? Awesome, since the smell of the garlic will consfuse the carrot fly keeping it away. Tomato and Cabbages, they are sworn enemies competing for nutrients and share pests.

So my setup is this:

| Row 1   | Row 2      | Row 2            | Row 3         | Row 4   | Row 5      | Row 6                | Row 7  | Row8    | Row 9 | Row 10  | Row 11   |
|---------|------------|------------------|---------------|---------|------------|----------------------|--------|---------|-------|---------|----------|
| Lettuce | Strawberry | Beet and Parsley | To Be Planted | Brocoli | Fabs Beans | Carrot and Coriander | Garlic | Cabbage | Onion | Lettuce | Spinach |


![A complete vegetable garden](/images/2026/completevegetablegardenoctober.jpg)

And Lasty I set up another corner just for my aromatic plants, some parley and coriander from seeds, basil, lavender, rosemary and thyme, all already in vases.

![A bunch of potted plants on top a concrete slab](/images/2026/aromaticplantzone.jpg)

I even took the liberty to grow some flowers, as you can see I´m trying to multiply my roses through some cut branches. And Calendulas alongside some Coneflowers still in seed. 

When they get big enough I plan on planting next to the vegetable garden and next to fruit trees. They have double benefits, for one thing they are edible flowers and have medicinal properties.

As well attract polinators and certain insects liked Lady bugs that feed off any aphids and other bugs that can plague a garden.


At the end of the month I got to see the plants I added starting taking quite nicely. But who nows what November will bring.

![A lettuce slowy growing and getting bigger by the day](/images/blog/alface.jpg)

![Calnedula  growing from small vases](/images/2026/calendulaseedlings.jpg)

It´s getting a bit colder around here and I might have to look into getting a thermal blanket for the less winter resistent plants to protect them from frost.

Fingers crossed if I manae to get them through the Winter season.
